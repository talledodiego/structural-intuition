# Vision

## Why this project exists

Structural mechanics is usually taught through equations. Students learn to apply formulas, but often without developing a feeling for *how* a structure responds: what happens if a column is twice as long, why a section works better rotated, why a material that is strong may still be the wrong choice.

Generative and agentic AI now make it cheap to build interactive tools. This project uses that opportunity to give students a set of small "laboratories" where a single concept can be explored freely, at any time.

> The value is not in generating answers, but in enabling exploration that builds intuition.

## Audience

Applets are designed for three audiences, in order of priority:

1. **Architecture students** — need sound structural conception, not detailed calculation. Mathematics is kept in the background.
2. **Urban planning students** — no technical background. Need qualitative understanding of why structures fail (e.g. in disaster contexts).
3. **Engineering students** — can use the same applets and go deeper through the "Why?" section and the code.

An applet should be usable by audience 1 without reading any equation, and still be correct and useful for audience 3.

## Principles

1. **One concept per applet.** Each applet carries one key message. If a second message emerges, it becomes a second applet.
2. **Predict in class, explore anywhere.** The teacher asks students to commit to an expectation in class (slides, live polls). The applet itself stays a free playground that students can reopen at home.
3. **Explore → observe → interpret.** The applet supports the full cycle, not just parameter sliders.
4. **Correct first.** Every applet is validated against known solutions. Simplifications are allowed, but must be declared.
5. **Conception over calculation.** Show trends, proportions and governing parameters, not only numbers.
6. **Zero friction.** Students open a link. No installation, no account.
7. **Open and reusable.** Code and content are openly licensed so that other teachers can use and adapt them, in Italian or English.

## What this project is not

- Not a design tool. Applets do not produce code-compliant verifications for real structures.
- Not a replacement for lectures. Applets support a lesson; they do not deliver it.
- Not a showcase of features. A simpler applet that students understand is better than a richer one they don't.
